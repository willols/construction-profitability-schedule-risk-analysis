# Construction Profitability and Schedule-Risk Analysis


## Purpose and scope:
- This report identifies which of the 18 active projects need management attention and why, using data available as of June 30, 2026. The 3 on-hold projects are excluded from this report.

### Profitability:
- Across all 18 active projects there is $25.23M in revised contract revenue and the forecast profit is $4.69M with a forecast profit margin of 18.58%.

### Budget risk:
- 9 of 18 active projects are forecast to have overruns, but all 18 are forecast to make a profit: meaning the forecast final cost remains below revised contract revenue.

### Schedule reliability:
- 9 of 18 have Unknown forecast delay days: 8 have outdated forecast finish dates that fall before their report dates and 1 has a missing forecast finish date. The other 9 forecasts are usable.

### Management takeaway:
- The active portfolio is forecast to be profitable overall, but the projects with forecast budget overruns, progress behind plan, or unknown finish forecasts need attention.


## Priority Projects:

### P093

Actual progress is 13.3 percentage points behind plan. Estimated remaining costs exceed the remaining budget, although no cost category has yet exceeded its revised budget based on incurred costs. All seven updates cite labor availability as the delay reason; the project manager should provide estimates to complete (ETC) by cost category, progress evidence, and staffing records to assess cost exposure and verify the reported schedule driver.

### P089

Reported completion decreased from 18.6% to 12.7% in the final update; this requires clarification before interpreting the wider gap as slower work alone. All three updates report labor availability as the delay reason. The project manager should clarify the completion decrease, verify crew availability and productivity, and assess a schedule recovery plan, using category-level ETC to evaluate its cost implications.

### P078

The progress gap jumped from 7.1 to 16.4 percentage points in the final update, while reported completion fell from 80.4% to 78.6%, triggering a decrease flag. Across its updates, forecast completion moved 23 days later, from July 27 to August 19, 2026. The final update cites owner decision/change order; the project manager should verify whether the progress decrease reflects a scope change or reporting correction and provide category-level ETC and progress estimates to investigate the forecast overrun.

### P083

The project manager should clarify the completion decrease, provide an updated finish forecast, and reassess remaining costs given the low forecast margin.

### P080

All updates cite labor availability as the delay reason, but this explanation remains unverified. ## Recommended Actions


### Schedule Reporting
- Management should ask project managers to provide updated finish forecasts for the nine projects with Unknown delays and explain reported completion decreases, including any scope changes or reporting corrections.

### Cost Forecasts
- Management should ask project managers to provide category-level ETC and supporting progress evidence to identify where remaining costs are expected to exceed available budgets.

### Recovery Planning:
- Project managers for the priority projects should verify reported constraints and evaluate recovery options, such as reallocating crews or resequencing work. They should estimate the time recovered, additional cost, and effect on forecast profit before recommending a plan for management approval.

### Additional Schedule Reviews
- Review P079 for its 29-day forecast delay despite forecast costs remaining below budget; P090 for its 14.9-percentage-point progress gap and outdated finish forecast; and P081 for its 22-day forecast delay and 14.4-percentage-point progress gap. These reviews supplement the five priority project reviews.


## Methodology and Limitations


### Methodology:
- The report covers 18 active projects as of June 30, 2026. For each project, the latest update with a report date on or before the cutoff was selected.
- Forecast final cost equals incurred non-payroll costs plus employee labor costs plus the estimate to complete from the selected project update, using the cleaned data layers.
- Revised contract revenue equals the original contract value plus approved change orders with approval dates on or before the cutoff.
- Forecast profit equals revised contract revenue minus forecast final cost.
- Forecast profit margin (%) equals forecast profit divided by revised contract revenue, multiplied by 100. Portfolio margin uses total forecast profit divided by total revised contract revenue, multiplied by 100.
- Forecast budget variance equals forecast final cost minus revised budget. Positive values indicate forecast overruns.
- Progress gap equals planned completion percentage minus actual completion percentage, measured in percentage points. Positive values indicate progress behind plan.
- Forecast delay equals forecast completion date minus baseline completion date, measured in days. Delays are shown as Unknown when the forecast completion date is missing or predates the selected update’s report date.
- The five review priorities were selected from projects with forecast budget overruns and schedule risk, indicated by a positive progress gap or a usable forecast delay greater than zero. Their review order reflects overrun size, forecast profit margin, schedule deterioration, and reporting concerns rather than a formal risk score.


### Limitations
- Unknown finish delays: Nine of the 18 active projects have Unknown finish delays: eight have forecast completion dates earlier than their latest update’s report date, and one has no forecast completion date. These forecasts cannot provide a usable estimate of finish delay; Unknown does not mean the projects are on schedule.
- Missing category-level ETC and progress: Estimates to complete are available only at the project level. Without category-level ETC and progress, forecast overruns cannot be reliably attributed to specific cost categories. Current category spending alone does not establish the cause.
- Unverified reporting: Reported delay reasons require confirmation. Decreases in reported completion also need investigation before being interpreted as lost progress or rework.
- Uncertainty in forecasts: Forecast profit and completion dates depend on estimates that may change. They represent expectations as of June 30, 2026, rather than guaranteed outcomes.
